# FAO conservation agriculture: three principles

This curator-written technical summary is based on the FAO Conservation
Agriculture overview. FAO describes conservation agriculture as a farming
system built around three principles: minimum mechanical soil disturbance,
permanent organic soil cover, and diversification of plant species through
crop sequences and associations.

The FAO overview describes minimum disturbance as direct seed or fertilizer
placement with no or minimal tillage. It describes permanent organic cover
through crop residues and/or cover crops, and species diversification through
varied rotations and associations. FAO presents these as a system-level
framework intended to support soil biological processes and resource-use
efficiency. This summary does not establish that the approach is suitable for
every crop, soil, farm, or Indian region.

Consult the linked FAO pages and local agronomic expertise before applying
practices.
