# FAO conservation agriculture: organic soil cover

This curator-written summary is based on FAO's technical page on soil organic
cover in conservation agriculture. FAO describes retaining crop residues on
the surface and using cover crops where there would otherwise be a long gap
between harvest and the next commercial crop.

FAO describes potential functions of cover crops including protecting soil
during fallow periods, adding organic matter, recycling or mobilizing
nutrients, and supporting soil structure and biodiversity. The page also
explains that surface mulch can reduce evaporation and increase infiltration,
with outcomes depending in part on the amount and type of cover. FAO notes
management trade-offs: cover crops use residual soil moisture and need to be
terminated or managed to avoid competition with the following crop.

These are general principles, not a crop-specific prescription. Suitability
depends on local climate, available water, species, and farm objectives.
