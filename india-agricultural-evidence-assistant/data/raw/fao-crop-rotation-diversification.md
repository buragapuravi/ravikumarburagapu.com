# FAO conservation agriculture: crop rotation and diversification

This curator-written summary is based on FAO's technical page on species
diversification in conservation agriculture. FAO describes crop rotation as
supporting soil biological diversity and allowing crops with different rooting
patterns to use different soil layers. The page also describes nutrient
recycling by rotation crops and the role of crop rotation in interrupting the
carry-over of crop-specific pests and diseases.

FAO lists potential rotation effects such as more diverse production, lower
risk of pest and weed infestations, different root channels through the soil
profile, and improved exploration of water and nutrients. The page stresses
that rotation design should reflect objectives such as food or fodder
production, residue supply, pest and weed management, and local soil and
climate conditions.

This is a general technical reference, not a locally validated cropping plan.
